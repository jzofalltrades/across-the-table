    How to play

    1. Lift a piece. You play white. Click one of your pieces and its legal squares glow green.
    2. Make your move. Click a green square. Any other square flashes red, just like the real board.
    3. Press the space bar. Use your keyboard, the amber Send move button, or the bar on the board itself. Changed your mind? Take back works until you press it.
    4. Watch the arm reply. It reaches across, picks up the opponent's piece and plays it. The amber squares show its move, and a red king means you're in check.
    5. Look around. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, switch cameras, or press Show electronics to see inside the platform.

    Commit, then send

    The 64 hall sensors can't tell when you've finished a move, so nothing is sent until you press the space bar. Take back works until then, like a hand still on the piece.

    Captures go first

    When the opponent captures, the arm clears your piece to the graveyard tray before making its own move. Castling moves king then rook. Promotion swaps in a queen.

    One grip, six heights

    The hook jaws close under each piece's collar (under the lip for rooks). The arm lowers to a height looked up from the piece type, from 35 mm for a pawn to 52 mm for a king.

    Inside the platform

    Press Show electronics. Under each square sits a WS2812 LED and a hall sensor on eight strip boards. Four 16-channel muxes feed the Pi, which runs the game and talks to the arm's ESP32 over UART. A 24 V supply feeds the stepper drivers, with a 5 V buck for the Pi and LEDs.

    What stands in for Lichess

    Here a small built-in engine plays the opponent. On the real board the Pi streams the opponent's moves from the Lichess Board API, and this same arm sequence plays them out.
